## Name how you decide, in your own world
- **Your company context. Name your company, your product, your market, and the main constraints you operate under.:** Company & Product: Meridian, an enterprise construction project-management platform that serves as the system of record for 38 of the top 100 US general contractors.

Market: Enterprise US general contractors managing large-scale, multi-site construction projects valued at $50M or more.

Main Constraints:
- Additive Foundations: All capabilities must build directly onto the existing core platform rather than initiating a multi-year architecture rebuild.
- Non-Negotiable Enterprise Governance: Core compliance, financial, and administrative workflows required by back-office teams cannot be altered or removed.
- Job-Site Competition: Mobile experience must directly compete with a foreman’s native phone camera and informal group text chains under an under-15-second interaction limit.
- Resource Boundary: Executing within a dedicated cross-functional team of 10 across product, mobile engineering, UX research, and customer success.
- **Strategy. How do you tell a real strategy from a list of goals in your world?:** Goals are specific targets we have identified for our company. They are the tangible outcomes we want to achieve as a company.

The strategy is the "HOW" we will execute to achieve the goal.
- **Prioritization. How do you separate what is loud from what is strategic in your actual backlog?:** Loud are items that SEEM like they would provide value or are requests from stakeholders that might make their lives easier but do not directly tie to our strategic goals nor do they contribute to the outcomes we seek to accomplish.

Strategic items in the backlog can be directly tied to OKRs and the objective goals/direction the company is headed. They are critical in enabling our profits, growth, and success as a company.
- **Teams. How do you approach a difficult conversation when someone's performance needs to change?:** Clarity is kindness. We need to have a defined rubric/criteria of expectations. If an individual is not performing as expected, the process should be to:
1. Identify the current behavior / performance
2. Compare it against the already defined expectation
3. Name the gap between current state and expectation
4. Create a plan to resolve the open gaps through changed actions and training.
- **Alignment. How do you hold a no, or bring a stakeholder to agreement, when priorities collide?:** Tangible, evidence based dialogue is key. Resources should not be re-allocated without confirmation that the new priority is higher than previously prioritized work. And the tradeoff to switch has been accepted by key stakeholders.

- **AI. Where do you let AI lead, where a human signs off, and what you never hand over?:** AI is a tool to help our humans work better and faster. ## Teach it the people you answer to
- **Who they are and their role:** Jane - Chief Revenue Officer & VP of Enterprise Sales

Focused on ARR, customer retention, and defending Meridian's market position across top 100 US General Contractors.
- **What they care about most:** 1. Enterprise Contract Value & Retention: Protecting Meridian’s system-of-record status across the 38 top-100 GCs and preventing churn in $50M+ projects.

2. Commercial Defensibility: Maintaining the "complexity premium" that allows Meridian to charge high enterprise contract values.   

3. Measurable ROI: Ensuring capital allocation yields explicit financial returns, contract expansions, or churn reduction rather than abstract UX improvements.
- **What they tend to push back on:** 1. Mobile Rebuilds & Platform Cannibalization: Anything that smells like a multi-year architecture rewrite or risks stripping away back-office compliance and governance depth.

2. Short-Term Feature Bloat: Adding low-yield administrative fields onto mobile surfaces that slow down app adoption.

3. Vague Product Metrics: Success metrics like "user delight," "intuitiveness," or simple login volume that don't directly correlate to contract renewals or operational efficiency.
- **What earns their yes:** 1. Additive Solutions: Clear execution plans that build on top of the existing core platform without altering enterprise compliance or finance workflows.

2. Frontline-to-Office Data Velocity: Showing how zero-friction mobile capture directly improves back-office data completeness, RFI resolution speed, and risk mitigation for project executives.

3. Rigorous Financial Models & Kill Criteria: Strategic bets backed by clear ROI calculations, explicit payback periods, and strict quarterly kill criteria before committing long-term resources.
