Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Field superintendents and foremen at the 38 top 100 US construction firms where Meridian is the system of record input their daily updates (photos, logs) directly into Meridian as their primary workflow. By leveraging our existing enterprise system-of-record position, we focus exclusively on frontline job-site adoption, creating zero-latency, intuitive mobile entry points that eliminate the friction driving teams to informal off-app channels.

The hard no

We choose not to add administrative governance, approval gates, or multi-field enterprise compliance forms onto job-site mobile surfaces. To win field mindshare against SMS and native photo galleries, the mobile app must remain exclusively optimized for under-15-second, frictionless data capture. Adding administrative overhead creates cognitive burden, slows down mobile performance, and reproduces the exact bureaucratic friction that forced field teams to default to informal text chains in the first place.

Objective and key results

Objective: Transform Meridian into the frictionless, primary daily entry point for frontline field teams, making field-captured data the real-time source of truth for back-office operations.
-- KR1: Increase the percentage of weekly active field superintendents and foremen at our 38 enterprise firms who log at least one field update (photo, daily log, or issue) directly in Meridian.
-- KR2: Reduce the average lag time between a field photo/observation being captured and its formal logging in back-office RFIs or daily reports.
-- KR3: Decrease the volume of back-office-initiated manual data entries and offline text/email attachments processed per active project.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

NOW (Q1 Horizon — Owned by Mobile Engineering & UX Research):
- Bet 1 (Job-Site Data Reliability): We bet eliminating offline connectivity dead zones will drive a 30% increase in daily mobile activity for field superintendents working in concrete shafts and underground site areas.
- Bet 2 (Frictionless Daily Reporting): We bet compressing daily logging into a four-field, under-15-second interaction will reduce off-app WhatsApp/SMS chatter by 40% for foremen on active job sites.
- Bet 3 (Visual-First Site Communication): We bet enabling native on-photo markups at the point of capture will cut the lag time between site issue identification and formal back-office RFI creation in half for field teams and project managers.

NEXT (Q2–Q3 Horizon — Owned by Product & Mobile Engineering):
- AI-Assisted RFI & Observation Drafting: Convert raw field photo markups into structured back-office RFI drafts automatically.
- Real-Time Push Alerts for Schedule Changes: Deliver instant, automated schedule change notifications to field superintendents.
- Procore Import/Export Integration Repair: Stabilize external data ingestion to bridge field updates seamlessly into legacy back-office workflows.
- Automated Compliance Checklist Generator: Streamline compliance tracking building on top of active daily log habits.
- Subcontractor Document Sharing Portal: Extend mobile touchpoints to external trade partners once internal field adoption is secure.

LATER (Future Horizon — Owned by Product & Customer Success):
- AI-Driven Predictive Risk & Weather Integration: Proactive safety and schedule risk mitigation powered by real-time job-site telemetry.
- Mid-Market Light-Mobile Onboarding Motion: Low-friction entry tier for contractors ($5M–$50M) without diluting enterprise governance.
- Subcontractor Performance Analytics: Automated contractor reliability scoring for C-suite risk teams.

Trade-off memo

1. Sequenced First (The Rocks): We prioritized Offline-First Mobile Sync (Item #1), Daily Log Simplification (Item #4), and Photo Markup Tools (Item #2). These directly eliminate job-site friction, targeting the under-15-second interaction limit required to win field mindshare back from WhatsApp and native camera rolls.

2. Pushed Out (Next/Later): We pushed out AI-Assisted RFI Drafting (Item #9) and Subcontractor Document Sharing (Item #14). While valuable, AI drafting requires consistent field photo logs as a prerequisite, and external subcontractor workflows must follow internal foreman adoption.

3. Cut Entirely (The Hard Nos): We explicitly rejected Executive Reporting Dashboards (Item #10), Creating a Separate Mobile App (Item #7), and Desktop UI Refreshes (Item #13). Executive dashboards feed top-down reporting without driving frontline adoption; a separate app violates our core constraint of additive architecture; and cosmetic desktop refreshes distract engineering capacity away from frontline job-site reliability.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

Mission: We own the frontline job-site experience for superintendents and foremen across Meridian’s 38 enterprise contractor accounts, driving primary daily mobile adoption by delivering zero-latency data capture and removing job-site workflow friction.

Where Ownership Stops:
- We do not own core back-office compliance workflows, financial ledgers, or enterprise ERP integrations, which remain under the core platform team.
- We do not own executive-level reporting analytics or custom C-suite dashboard customization.
- We do not own desktop-side administrative settings or web application infrastructure.

How we decide

Decision Ownership:
- Product Leader: Owns strategic trade-offs, roadmap prioritization, backlog scoping (Rocks vs. Hard Nos), and final say on feature inclusion.
- Engineering Lead: Owns technical architecture, offline sync data integrity, performance SLA adherence, and delivery estimates.
- Design/UX Lead: Owns user interaction patterns, ensuring all job-site mobile workflows adhere strictly to the under-15-second interaction rule.

Conflict Resolution Framework:
- Strategic Anchor: Disagreements are evaluated against our primary objective: Does this directly drive frontline mobile logging and eliminate off-app shadow workflows under job-site conditions?
- Evidence-Based Arbitration: If technical feasibility and user experience collide, the team conducts a 48-hour field test with active superintendents to gather real-world usage data before committing.
- Escalation Path: If alignment cannot be reached within the 10-person cross-functional squad, the Product Leader makes the final binding decision, documenting the trade-off, risk, and rationale for executive stakeholders.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

Investing in an offline-first mobile architecture directly protects Meridian’s $50M+ ARR enterprise revenue base by eliminating the job-site technical failures that drive frontline superintendents to default to off-app WhatsApp chains. When field teams stop logging daily site updates, back-office data completeness breaks, triggering audit gaps and contract churn across our 38 top-100 enterprise accounts. By removing connectivity friction and compressing daily logging into a 15-second mobile workflow, we capture complete field telemetry, defending high-ARR contract renewals against lightweight mobile competitors and unlocking expansion seats across active job sites without incurring high-cost platform rebuilds.

Kill criterion

If weekly active field superintendents across our target enterprise accounts have not reached a 20% adoption baseline (logging at least one native daily entry or photo observation per week) by the end of Q3 (September 30), the offline mobile feature investment will be paused immediately, and Q4 engineering resources will be reallocated to back-office automation before committing additional capital.

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Key learnings

Leverage the frameworks, tools, and guideline provided in the course.

Aha moment

It's not an ambiguous, conceptual, buzzword. It can be structured, evidence-driven, and enable the bigger picture and broader company roadmap/direction.
